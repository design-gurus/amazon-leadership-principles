# Strive to be Earth's Best Employer

Strive to be Earth's Best Employer means making the working environment safer, more productive, and more inclusive, and caring about the growth of colleagues. It is one of the two principles that Amazon added later than the first 14, and it is asked more often of managers and senior candidates.

## What the interviewer listens for

- A problem in how the team worked: an exhausting on-call rotation, a slow onboarding process, or unequal access to good assignments.
- An action that the candidate took personally, with or without formal authority.
- Attention to individual people, shown through listening before acting.
- A measured change in the team's experience, such as fewer night pages or faster onboarding.

## Questions

1. Tell me about a time you improved your team's working conditions.
2. Describe a time you supported a colleague who was struggling.
3. Tell me about a time you made your team more inclusive.
4. How have you helped a teammate grow in their career?
5. Tell me about a time you noticed that a teammate was overloaded. What did you do?
6. Describe a time you changed a process to make your team's work more sustainable.

## Weak and strong answers

| Answer | What it contains |
|--------|------------------|
| Weak | A social event organized for the team, or a general statement about valuing people |
| Strong | A specific burden on the team, what the candidate learned by asking, the change made, and a number that shows the improvement |

## Sample answer

> "Our on-call engineers were receiving about 45 pages a week, and two teammates told me in private that they were considering leaving because of interrupted sleep. I was not the manager, but I asked to lead a review. I categorized four weeks of pages and found that 60 percent came from three alerts that required no action. I removed those alerts with the team's agreement, wrote instructions for the five most frequent remaining ones, and proposed that night pages go to a second region's team during their daytime. Pages fell to 12 a week, and night pages fell from 14 a month to 2. Both teammates stayed, and our next internal survey score for workload rose from 2.9 to 4.1 out of 5."

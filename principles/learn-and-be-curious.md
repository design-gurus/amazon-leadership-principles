# Learn and Be Curious

Learn and Be Curious means continuing to learn throughout a career and exploring ideas beyond the current assignment. In an interview, the strongest evidence is a skill the candidate acquired because the work required it, followed by a result that depended on that skill.

## What the interviewer listens for

- A specific gap in knowledge, stated honestly.
- The method used to learn: documentation, source code, a course, or a colleague in another team.
- The speed of learning, expressed in days or weeks.
- An outcome that would not have happened without the new knowledge.

## Questions

1. Tell me about something you had to learn quickly.
2. Describe a time you took on work outside your expertise.
3. Tell me about a time your curiosity led to an improvement.
4. What is the most useful thing you learned in the past year, and how did you apply it?
5. Tell me about a time you asked a question that changed a project's direction.
6. Describe a time you learned from a failure.

## Weak and strong answers

| Answer | What it contains |
|--------|------------------|
| Weak | A list of courses or books, or a technology studied out of interest with no effect on any work |
| Strong | The gap, the learning method, the time it took, and a result at work that used the new skill |

## Sample answer

> "I am a backend engineer, and our team inherited a billing dashboard that loaded in nine seconds. Nobody on the team knew the frontend framework it used, and the only frontend engineer had left. I spent two evenings on the framework's official tutorial and then read our dashboard's code with its profiler open. I learned that every table cell triggered a separate render. I asked a frontend engineer on another team to review my first change, which took her 20 minutes. Within two weeks I had reduced the load time to 1.4 seconds. I then ran a one-hour session for my team, and two other engineers have shipped dashboard changes since."

# Have Backbone; Disagree and Commit

Have Backbone; Disagree and Commit means challenging a decision respectfully when one disagrees with it, even when the other person is senior. Once the decision is final, it means supporting that decision completely, whether or not the candidate's view prevailed.

## What the interviewer listens for

- Disagreement with a manager or a senior engineer. A disagreement with a peer is usually considered too easy.
- An argument based on data, presented directly to the person concerned.
- A clear ending: the candidate persuaded the other person, or lost and committed.
- For the commit half: real effort on the chosen plan, with no reminder afterwards that the candidate had objected.

## Questions

1. Tell me about a time you disagreed with your manager.
2. Describe a time you challenged a decision made by someone senior.
3. Tell me about a time you committed to a decision you did not agree with.
4. Tell me about an unpopular position you defended.
5. Give an example of a time you were overruled. What did you do next?
6. Describe a time you spoke up when it was uncomfortable to do so.

## Weak and strong answers

| Answer | What it contains |
|--------|------------------|
| Weak | A minor disagreement with a peer, a story in which the candidate stayed silent, or one in which the candidate continued to resist after the decision |
| Strong | A senior person, the evidence presented, the decision, and full commitment with a result |

## Sample answer

> "My manager wanted to rewrite our order service in a new language and estimated four months. I believed that an incremental refactor would take two months and carry less risk. I wrote a one-page comparison with defect data from our last rewrite, which had run 10 weeks late, and presented it to him and to the principal engineer. They chose the rewrite, mainly because hiring for the old language had become difficult, a factor I had weighted too low. I said that I still saw the schedule risk and that I would commit. I then volunteered to build the parallel-run test tool that compared old and new responses. It caught 37 differences before launch, and the rewrite shipped two weeks late with no customer-facing defects."

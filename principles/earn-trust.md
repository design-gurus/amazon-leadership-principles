# Earn Trust

Earn Trust means being honest, listening carefully, and admitting one's own mistakes even when that is uncomfortable. Interviewers most often test it with a question about a mistake or a failure, so a candidate without a real failure story cannot answer it.

## What the interviewer listens for

- A real mistake that belonged to the candidate, not to the team or to circumstances.
- Fast and direct disclosure to the people affected.
- Repair of the damage and a change that prevents a repeat.
- For feedback questions: criticism that the candidate accepted and acted on.

## Questions

1. Tell me about a mistake you made.
2. Describe a time you received critical feedback. What did you do with it?
3. Tell me about a time you had to deliver bad news.
4. Tell me about a time you had to rebuild a working relationship.
5. Give an example of a time you disagreed with a colleague and kept their trust.
6. Tell me about a time you missed a commitment.

## Weak and strong answers

| Answer | What it contains |
|--------|------------------|
| Weak | A strength presented as a weakness, a mistake with no consequence, or blame placed on another person |
| Strong | The mistake in one clear sentence, its cost, how quickly the candidate reported it, the repair, and the permanent change |

## Sample answer

> "I ran a data cleanup script against production that I had tested only on a sample. It removed the saved preferences of about 2,300 users, because my filter matched a second account type that I had not checked. I noticed within 20 minutes, stopped the script, and posted in our incident channel that I had caused it and what was affected. I restored the rows from the previous night's backup in three hours, and 60 users lost the changes they had made that day. I wrote to our support lead with the list so that they could contact those users. I then added a mandatory dry-run mode to our script template, and every cleanup now needs a second reviewer."

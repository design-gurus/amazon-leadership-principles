# Think Big

Think Big means proposing a direction that is larger than the immediate request and explaining why the larger version is worth the effort. The principle does not reward size alone. It rewards a candidate who saw a wider opportunity and convinced others with reasoning and evidence.

## What the interviewer listens for

- A narrow request that the candidate recognized as one case of a wider problem.
- A proposal with a clear argument: who benefits, by how much, and at what cost.
- A first step small enough to start, so that the large idea did not remain a document.
- The result so far, including any part of the vision that has not yet been reached.

## Questions

1. Tell me about a time you proposed a bold idea.
2. Describe a time you looked beyond the immediate task and saw a larger opportunity.
3. Tell me about your most ambitious project.
4. Give an example of a time you set a goal that others thought was too high.
5. Tell me about a time you convinced others to adopt a long-term vision.
6. Describe a time a narrow solution would have worked, and you chose a wider one. Why?

## Weak and strong answers

| Answer | What it contains |
|--------|------------------|
| Weak | A large project that was assigned to the candidate, or an ambitious idea with no first step and no result |
| Strong | The small request, the larger opportunity behind it, the argument used to gain support, and a measured first result |

## Sample answer

> "My team was asked to add rate limiting to one public API after a customer overloaded it. While designing it, I counted 11 other teams that had each written their own rate limiter, with four different behaviors on rejection. I wrote a two-page proposal for a shared rate limiting library and service, and estimated that it would remove about 6,000 lines of duplicated code. My director approved one quarter. I delivered our own API first, in three weeks, to prove the design. By the end of the quarter, seven teams had adopted it, and the number of overload incidents across those services fell from nine in the previous quarter to two."

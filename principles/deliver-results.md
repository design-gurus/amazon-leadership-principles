# Deliver Results

Deliver Results means finishing the work that matters, on time and at the required quality, despite obstacles. Every answer for this principle ends with a number, and the strongest ones describe an obstacle that nearly prevented delivery.

## What the interviewer listens for

- A goal that was defined in advance, with a date and a measure.
- An obstacle: a lost team member, a changed requirement, or a failed dependency.
- The specific actions the candidate took to recover, including what was removed from the scope.
- The final result against the original goal, stated honestly even when it was a partial success.

## Questions

1. Tell me about a goal you nearly missed. How did you recover?
2. Describe your most significant professional achievement.
3. Tell me about a time you delivered a project under a tight deadline.
4. Give an example of a time you had too many priorities. How did you choose?
5. Tell me about a time you did not meet a goal.
6. Describe a time you had to overcome a major obstacle to finish a project.

## Weak and strong answers

| Answer | What it contains |
|--------|------------------|
| Weak | A description of effort and long hours, a result without a number, or a project that was never difficult |
| Strong | The goal, the obstacle, the recovery decisions, and the result compared with the target |

## Sample answer

> "I led the migration of our reporting database to a new cluster. The contract on the old hardware ended in 10 weeks, and my plan needed eight. In week three, the second engineer on the project left the company. I listed the 64 reports and checked their usage, and 22 of them had not been opened in six months. I obtained written agreement from the three teams that owned them to retire those reports. I also automated the validation that compared old and new results, which had been taking a day per report. We completed the migration of the remaining 42 reports four days before the contract ended, with two reports needing a correction in the first week."

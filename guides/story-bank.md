# Building a Story Bank

A story bank is a prepared set of true stories from your own work, each one written in the [STAR format](star-method.md) and mapped to the principles it can support. A bank of 8 to 10 stories is enough for a full Amazon loop, and 6 or 7 strong ones will answer most of the questions.

## Start from your stories, not from the list

A common mistake is to read the 16 principles and try to write one story for each. That method produces 16 thin stories, and several of them fail on the first follow-up question. Work in the other direction: list your real projects first, then ask which principles each one shows.

## Step 1: List the raw material

Review the last three to five years of your work and write one line for each of these:

- Two projects that you are proud of.
- One project that failed or missed its goal.
- One mistake that was yours.
- One disagreement with a manager or a senior engineer.
- One problem you fixed that was not assigned to you.
- One time you investigated data and found something unexpected.
- One time you worked inside a hard limit on time, people, or budget.
- One time you helped another person improve.

This list usually produces 8 to 10 candidates.

## Step 2: Map each story to the principles

For every story, mark only the principles that it truly shows. A story shows Dive Deep when you examined the data yourself, not because the project involved a database. The sample grid below belongs to a hypothetical senior engineer.

| Story | Primary principle | Also supports |
|-------|-------------------|---------------|
| Export timeouts found in support tickets | Customer Obsession | Dive Deep, Deliver Results |
| Silent backup failure on another team's job | Ownership | Bias for Action, Earn Trust |
| Release checklist cut from 23 steps to 5 | Invent and Simplify | Frugality, Deliver Results |
| Latency traced to the wrong suspected cause | Are Right, A Lot | Dive Deep, Learn and Be Curious |
| Cleanup script that deleted user preferences | Earn Trust | Ownership, Insist on the Highest Standards |
| Rewrite decision lost to the manager | Have Backbone; Disagree and Commit | Are Right, A Lot, Deliver Results |
| Shared rate limiter proposed for 11 teams | Think Big | Invent and Simplify, Frugality |
| Mentoring a new graduate to promotion | Hire and Develop the Best | Strive to be Earth's Best Employer |
| On-call pages reduced from 45 to 12 a week | Strive to be Earth's Best Employer | Dive Deep, Ownership |
| Contact upload redesigned for privacy | Success and Scale Bring Broad Responsibility | Customer Obsession, Insist on the Highest Standards |

## Step 3: Read the grid

Three things become visible once the grid is complete.

- **Your strongest stories.** One or two stories will support four or five principles. Know these best.
- **Your thin principles.** These are principles that no story supports, or that only one story supports as a secondary point. In the sample grid, Bias for Action and Learn and Be Curious each appear once in the last column, so that engineer should prepare a dedicated story for each.
- **Your overused stories.** A story marked for eight principles is probably being stretched. Interviewers compare notes, and a story told three times will be noticed.

## Step 4: Fill the gaps

There are three ways to fill a thin principle, in order of preference.

1. Find a smaller real example. An afternoon spent reading logs instead of trusting a dashboard is a valid Dive Deep answer.
2. Tell an existing story with a different emphasis. A migration is a Deliver Results answer when it opens with the deadline, and a Bias for Action answer when it opens with the decision to start early.
3. Give an honest adjacent answer. A candidate who has never hired anyone can describe mentoring or interviewing.

## The four principles candidates most often lack

| Principle | The story it requires |
|-----------|-----------------------|
| [Have Backbone; Disagree and Commit](../principles/have-backbone-disagree-and-commit.md) | A disagreement with someone senior, not with a peer |
| [Dive Deep](../principles/dive-deep.md) | A summary that was wrong, found by examining the data |
| [Earn Trust](../principles/earn-trust.md) | A real mistake with a real cost |
| [Frugality](../principles/frugality.md) | A limit you worked inside, not a request for more resources |

# Leadership Principles by Role

Every Amazon candidate is interviewed against the same 16 principles, but the emphasis and the expected size of the evidence change with the role and the level. This page summarizes what candidates commonly report. It is not an official mapping, and any interviewer may ask about any principle.

## Emphasis by role

| Role | Principles commonly emphasized | Why |
|------|--------------------------------|-----|
| Software engineer (entry and mid level) | Customer Obsession, Ownership, Learn and Be Curious, Dive Deep, Deliver Results, Bias for Action | The interview looks for reliable execution, fast learning, and care for the user |
| Senior engineer | Ownership, Dive Deep, Insist on the Highest Standards, Have Backbone; Disagree and Commit, Are Right, A Lot, Invent and Simplify | The interview looks for technical judgment and influence over a team's decisions |
| Principal engineer | Think Big, Are Right, A Lot, Earn Trust, Have Backbone; Disagree and Commit, Success and Scale Bring Broad Responsibility | The interview looks for direction set across several teams and for long-term consequences |
| Engineering manager | Hire and Develop the Best, Earn Trust, Deliver Results, Strive to be Earth's Best Employer, Think Big, Frugality | The interview looks for results achieved through other people |

## The same principle at three levels

The principle stays the same, and the expected scope grows. Scope here means the number of people and systems that the story affected. The table uses Ownership as the example.

| Level | An answer of suitable scope |
|-------|-----------------------------|
| Mid-level engineer | Fixed a failing job that belonged to a neighboring team and added an alert |
| Senior engineer | Noticed a recurring class of failure across a team's services and led the work to remove it |
| Principal engineer or manager | Identified a gap that no team owned, obtained agreement from several teams, and established a permanent owner |

A story that is too small for the level is a common reason for an offer at a lower level than the one the candidate applied for.

## Software engineers

Most interviews combine a coding or design problem with behavioral questions. Prepare technical stories with enough detail to answer three levels of follow-up questions, because Dive Deep is examined through the candidate's own project.

## Senior and principal engineers

Expect questions about disagreement with senior people, decisions made with incomplete information, and work that crossed team boundaries. Stories should name the number of teams involved and the duration of the effect.

- [Grokking Staff Engineer Interview](https://www.designgurus.io/course/grokking-staff-engineer-interview?utm_source=github&utm_medium=repo&utm_campaign=amazon-leadership-principles&utm_content=guides-principles-by-role) covers scope, technical strategy, and influence without authority.
- The free [Staff Engineer Interview](https://github.com/design-gurus/staff-engineer-interview) repository summarizes what changes at that level.

## Engineering managers

Expect questions about hiring, underperformance, team health, and delivery through a team. The answers still use "I", and the actions are those of a manager: setting a standard, giving feedback, and making a staffing decision.

- [Grokking the Engineering Manager Interview](https://www.designgurus.io/course/grokking-the-engineering-manager-interview?utm_source=github&utm_medium=repo&utm_campaign=amazon-leadership-principles&utm_content=guides-principles-by-role) covers people leadership, situational, and project retrospective questions.
- The free [Engineering Manager Interview](https://github.com/design-gurus/engineering-manager-interview) repository lists the rounds and a question bank.

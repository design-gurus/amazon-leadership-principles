# Common Mistakes in Amazon Behavioral Answers

Most weak answers fail for one of ten reasons. Each mistake below is followed by its correction. Check every prepared story against this list before the interview.

| # | Mistake | Correction |
|---|---------|------------|
| 1 | Saying "we" throughout the answer | Describe the team in one sentence, then state what you did with "I" |
| 2 | Ending without a number | Finish with a measured result, a count, or a comparison with the goal |
| 3 | Spending a minute on the background | Limit the Situation and Task to three sentences |
| 4 | Answering with a hypothetical ("I would...") | Describe one real event. The question asks what you did |
| 5 | Naming the principle aloud | Place the evidence in the answer and let the interviewer identify the principle |
| 6 | Repeating one story across interviews | Prepare 8 to 10 stories and record which one each interviewer has heard |
| 7 | Presenting a failure that cost nothing | Choose a real mistake, state its cost, and describe the permanent change |
| 8 | Using a peer disagreement for Have Backbone | Prepare a disagreement with a manager or a senior engineer |
| 9 | Blaming another person or team | State the facts neutrally and describe your own part |
| 10 | Failing on the second follow-up question | Prepare each story three questions deep, including the technical detail |

## Why these mistakes cost more at Amazon

Interviewers record what the candidate says and submit written feedback under each assigned principle. A vague answer leaves the interviewer with nothing to write, and an empty section counts against the candidate in the debrief, the meeting where the decision is made. The [loop guide](how-the-amazon-loop-works.md) explains this process.

## Three mistakes in detail

**The hypothetical answer.** A candidate who has no story sometimes describes what they would do. Interviewers usually interrupt and ask for a real example. It is better to say "I have not faced exactly that, and the closest situation was..." and then describe a real event.

**The failure that cost nothing.** "I work too hard" and "the project was late by one day" do not satisfy an Earn Trust question. The interviewer is looking for honesty about a real loss, followed by what the candidate changed. The [Earn Trust](../principles/earn-trust.md) page contains an example.

**The stretched story.** A story that truly shows Deliver Results does not also show Frugality merely because the project had a budget. When the evidence for the principle is not in the story, the follow-up questions reveal that within a minute. The [story bank guide](story-bank.md) explains how to find a gap and fill it honestly.

## A final check for each story

- The story is true, and it happened to you.
- The Action section is at least half of the answer.
- The Result contains a number that you can explain.
- You can name the alternative you rejected and the reason.
- You can state what you would do differently.

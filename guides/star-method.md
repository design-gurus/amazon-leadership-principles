# The STAR Method

STAR is a four-part structure for answering a behavioral question, which is a question about something the candidate actually did in the past. The letters stand for Situation, Task, Action, and Result. Amazon interviewers are trained to collect evidence in this shape, so an answer that follows it is easier for them to record.

## The four parts

| Part | What it answers | Share of the answer |
|------|-----------------|---------------------|
| Situation | Where were you, and what was happening? | About 15 percent |
| Task | What were you responsible for, and what made it difficult? | About 10 percent |
| Action | What did you personally do, in order? | About 60 percent |
| Result | What changed, and by how much? | About 15 percent |

A complete answer takes about two minutes when spoken. The most common fault is a Situation that runs for a full minute and leaves no time for the Action.

## Why the Result needs a number

The interviewer must write evidence that other people will read in the debrief, which is the meeting where the hiring decision is made. "The system became faster" cannot be compared with anything. "The 95th percentile latency fell from 1.8 seconds to 600 milliseconds" can be recorded and defended.

When no exact number exists, three substitutes are acceptable:

- An honest estimate with its basis: "about 30 percent, based on the two weeks before and after".
- A count: tickets, incidents, teams, hours, or steps.
- A comparison with the goal: "four days before the deadline".

Never invent a number. Follow-up questions usually ask how it was measured.

## Use "I", not "we"

The interviewer is evaluating one person. An Action section full of "we decided" and "we built" gives no evidence about the candidate. Describe the team's work in one sentence, then state what you did yourself.

## Prepare for follow-up questions

Amazon interviewers ask follow-up questions until they reach the limit of what the candidate knows. Common ones are listed below.

| Follow-up question | What it tests |
|--------------------|---------------|
| "What exactly was your part?" | Whether the work was yours |
| "Why did you choose that option?" | The reasoning and the alternatives |
| "How did you measure that?" | Whether the number is real |
| "What would you do differently?" | Reflection and learning |
| "What did your manager think?" | Whether there was real disagreement or risk |
| "What happened afterwards?" | Whether the result lasted |

For each story, write the answers to these six questions in advance. A story that fails on the second follow-up is weaker than a modest story that holds through all six.

## A short template

1. Situation: one or two sentences with the team, the system, and the scale.
2. Task: one sentence with your responsibility and the constraint.
3. Action: three to five steps, each beginning with "I".
4. Result: one number, then one sentence on what changed permanently.

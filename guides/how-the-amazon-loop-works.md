# How the Amazon Loop Works

The loop is Amazon's name for the final interview stage, in which a candidate meets several interviewers in one day or across a few days. This page describes the process as candidates and former interviewers commonly report it. Amazon does not publish every detail, and teams differ, so treat the numbers below as typical and not as official.

## The stages before the loop

| Stage | What usually happens |
|-------|----------------------|
| Online assessment | For many engineering roles, a timed coding test, sometimes with a work-style questionnaire based on the principles |
| Phone screen | One or two interviews of 45 to 60 minutes, with a technical problem and one or two behavioral questions |
| Loop | Four to six interviews of 45 to 60 minutes each |

## What happens in each loop interview

Each interviewer is commonly reported to be assigned two or three Leadership Principles in advance. The assignments overlap very little, so the loop as a whole covers most of the 16 principles.

For an engineering role, a typical interview combines a technical part with a behavioral part. An interviewer may spend 15 to 25 minutes on behavioral questions and the remaining time on coding or system design. For a manager role, the behavioral share is larger.

Interviewers take detailed notes, often typing while the candidate speaks. They are recording what the candidate said, as closely as possible, because the notes are the evidence used later.

## The Bar Raiser

A Bar Raiser is an interviewer from outside the hiring team who has received additional training for the role. The hiring manager has an urgent need to fill the position, and the Bar Raiser does not, which is the reason the role exists.

Three facts about the Bar Raiser are commonly reported:

- The Bar Raiser can veto a hire, even when the hiring manager wants the candidate.
- The Bar Raiser judges whether the candidate is stronger than half of the people currently in that role at that level.
- Candidates are usually not told which interviewer is the Bar Raiser. This interview is often mostly behavioral and contains many follow-up questions.

## The written feedback and the debrief

After the interviews, each interviewer writes feedback independently. The feedback lists the evidence for each assigned principle and ends with a vote to hire or not to hire. Interviewers are expected to submit it before they see the opinions of the others.

The interviewers then meet in a debrief, which is the meeting that produces the decision. They read the written feedback and discuss the evidence principle by principle. The Bar Raiser usually leads this meeting.

## What this means for preparation

| Fact about the loop | What to do |
|---------------------|------------|
| Interviewers write down what you say | Give specific sentences and numbers that can be quoted |
| Principles are divided among interviewers | Prepare evidence for all 16, not only the common ones |
| Interviewers compare notes in the debrief | Do not repeat one story across interviews |
| The Bar Raiser asks many follow-up questions | Know every story two or three questions deep |
| Feedback is organized by principle | Make the evidence for the principle easy to identify in each answer |
